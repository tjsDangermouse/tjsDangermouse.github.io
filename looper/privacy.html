Looper for iPhone and Apple Watch

Privacy policy

Last updated: 20 September 2026

Looper uses your location to plan and guide walks and runs. It has no accounts, advertising, cross-app tracking, or analytics, and it does not sell personal information.

Location and route planning

Routes are calculated on your iPhone. Looper does not send your starting point, waypoints, planned routes, live position, or recorded track to the developer's servers.

To obtain nearby walking-path data, the app sends the bounds of the required map area to one of several public OpenStreetMap Overpass API providers. The request also reveals your IP address to that provider. Looper stores the downloaded path data in its private storage so it can be reused and lets you clear it in Settings.

Maps

The iPhone app downloads map styles and tiles from OpenFreeMap. Those requests necessarily reveal your IP address and the map area requested to OpenFreeMap and its delivery providers. OpenFreeMap says it uses no cookies or tracking technologies, does not store IP addresses in normal server logs, and may temporarily log them during a security incident. Read the OpenFreeMap privacy policy.

Map tiles downloaded for offline guidance are stored on your iPhone for the current outing and removed when it ends. Apple supplies the standard map shown by the Watch app; data Apple processes is governed by Apple's privacy policy.

Apple Health and workouts

If you grant permission, Looper saves completed walks and runs, including their route and workout measurements, to Apple Health. On Apple Watch it may read heart rate, walking or running distance, and active energy for the workout so it can show progress and save a complete workout.

Health and workout information is handled on your iPhone, Apple Watch, and in Apple Health. Looper does not send it to the developer's servers, use it for advertising, or share it with data brokers. You can change Looper's Health access at any time in the Health app or Settings.

Information kept on your devices

Looper stores information needed to provide its features, including:

  • app settings, pace preferences, and voice choice;
  • your last route and routes you mark as favourites;
  • the current outing, so it can recover after an interruption;
  • workout and navigation state shared between your paired iPhone and Apple Watch; and
  • temporary navigation and routing diagnostics used during testing.

This information is stored in the app's private storage. Diagnostic files leave your device only if you deliberately export and share them using the system share sheet. Deleting the app removes its local information, while workouts already saved in Apple Health remain under your control there.

What Looper does not do

  • No account or sign-in is required.
  • No advertising or marketing SDK is included.
  • No data is used to track you across apps or websites.
  • No personal information is sold.
  • No Health information is used for advertising or data mining.

Your choices

You can decline or revoke location and Health permissions in Settings. Some route planning, navigation, map, or workout features will then be unavailable, but Looper does not require an account and holds no account record for you to delete.

Children

Looper is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect account or contact information from them. Its location and workout features use the same device permissions and practices described above for every user.
